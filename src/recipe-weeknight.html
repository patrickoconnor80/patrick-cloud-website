Weeknight Skillet Dinners

ChickenBeefOne panUnder 30 min

Three sauced skillet dinners for nights you have half an hour. Serve over rice.

From Cooking with Chris: spicy chicken teriyaki, steak stir fry and honey chipotle chicken. The last two come from his weeknight-dinners thread. Its maple dijon and Mediterranean salmon are already on the marinades page, and its easy pho is the pho bowl.

At a glance

25 minutes, one pan

Per serving, without rice. A cup of white rice adds about 205 kcal and 45 g of carbs. You can add it in the full nutrition panel.

What makes them work

  • Slice the meat thin, against the grain
  • Sear in batches without crowding the pan
  • Add the sauce last and reduce it until it clings

Good to know

1Spicy chicken teriyaki

Serves 3 · 25 min

  • 1–1½ lbchicken thighs or breast, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbspcornstarch (coating)
  • 1 tbspavocado oil
  • ⅓ cupsoy sauce
  • 3 tbspbrown sugar
  • 2 tbsphoney
  • 2 tbspmirin
  • 1 tbsprice vinegar
  • 1 tspsesame oil
  • 1 tbspgochujang
  • ½ tsp + 1 tbsp watercornstarch slurry
  • 2 clovesgarlic, minced (optional)
  • 1 tspginger, minced (optional)
  • to servesesame seeds
  • to servegreen onions
  1. Whisk the soy sauce, brown sugar, honey, mirin, rice vinegar, sesame oil and gochujang (plus garlic and ginger if using). In another bowl, mix the slurry.
  2. Toss the chicken in the cornstarch, shake off the excess, and cook in a single layer in hot oil over medium-high heat until golden and almost done, 3–5 minutes a side.
  3. Pour in the sauce and simmer 1–2 minutes. Stir in the slurry. The sauce goes glossy in 30–60 seconds. Simmer 1–2 minutes more until it clings, adding a splash of water if it gets too thick.
  4. Serve over rice with sesame seeds and green onions.

The numbers assume 1¼ lb of chicken (about 15 oz cooked). USDA has no mirin or gochujang, so both are estimated from their main ingredients.

2Steak stir fry

Serves 4 · 25 min

  • 1 cupsoy sauce or coconut aminos
  • 4 tspfish sauce
  • to tastegarlic, grated
  • to tasteginger, grated
  • 2–4 tbspsriracha
  • 4 tbsphoney
  • 2 lbskirt or flank steak, sliced thin against the grain
  • 1 cupshiitake mushrooms
  • 1 cupcarrots, diced
  • 1 cupbroccoli florets
  • 2 tbspavocado oil
  • to servegreen onions
  • to servesesame seeds
  1. Mix the sauce and marinate the steak for a few minutes.
  2. Sear the steak in hot oil for only 2–3 minutes, because it goes back in later. Set it aside and discard the excess marinade.
  3. Add a splash more oil and cook the vegetables until done the way you like them.
  4. Return the steak, add the sauce, and cook a few minutes until it thickens and coats everything. Garnish with green onions and sesame seeds and serve over rice. Any protein or vegetables work.

Add a pinch of salt only if using coconut aminos. The thread doesn't say how much sauce is held back, so the numbers assume three-quarters of it is eaten. The garlic and ginger amounts (4 cloves, 1 tbsp) are mine.

3Honey chipotle chicken

Serves 3 · 20 min

  • 1½ lbchicken breasts or thighs, diced
  • to tastesalt
  • to tastepepper
  • to tastesmoked paprika
  • to tastegarlic powder
  • to tasteonion powder
  • to tastechipotles in adobo, diced (or just the sauce)
  • 2–3 tbspraw honey
  • 3–4garlic cloves
  • 1–2 tbspextra virgin olive oil
  • pinchsalt
  1. Toss the diced chicken with salt, pepper, smoked paprika, garlic powder and onion powder.
  2. Blend or stir the sauce: chipotles or adobo, honey, garlic, olive oil and a pinch of salt.
  3. Cook the chicken in a pan until almost done, then add the sauce and cook until it thickens and the chicken is cooked through.

The 1½ lb of chicken and the seasoning amounts are mine (about 1 tsp each, ¾ tsp salt, 2 tbsp chipotle). Canned chili peppers stand in for chipotles in adobo, so the real sodium is a little higher.

Notes

What the panel shows, in plain terms.

Soy sauce is the sodium

The steak stir fry uses a full cup of soy sauce. Even with a quarter thrown out with the marinade, that's most of the 3,000-plus mg of sodium per serving. Coconut aminos, which the thread offers as an alternative, are lower in sodium. If you use them, add the pinch of salt the recipe calls for. Whether that much sodium matters depends on the rest of your day. The Sodium page has the case that most people worry about it too much.

The sauces carry the sugar

Teriyaki gets its gloss from sugar. Brown sugar, honey, mirin and gochujang together come to about 30 g of sugar per serving, roughly 2½ tablespoons. The stir fry and honey chipotle sauces are lighter, at about 15 g, roughly a tablespoon. Adding the sauce at the end, as all three recipes do, also keeps the sugar off the high-heat sear, which matters for the reason on the marinades page.

Oils

The cooking fat is avocado oil, which is fine if it's genuine (see the purity problem). The teaspoon of sesame oil in the teriyaki is about 40% linoleic acid, but split three ways it's well under a gram per serving. As on the marinades page, the meat matters more: chicken thigh brings more omega-6 than everything else in the pan combined, and breast or steak brings less.
