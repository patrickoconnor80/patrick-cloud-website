Testosterone

Health GoalMen's healthHormonesEnergy & libido

Average testosterone in American men has fallen over the past few decades, and the drop isn't explained by men simply being older or heavier. Most of what's in your control is ordinary: sleep, body fat, eating enough, and a few nutrients. Most "T boosters" do nothing.

A high-T week

  • 7–9 hours in bed, same wake time daily
  • Lift heavy 2–3 times, walk daily
  • Eggs, red meat, butter, full-fat dairy; don't fear fat
  • Oysters or red meat for zinc
  • Sun on your skin, or D3 in winter if you test low
  • Glass and steel for hot food and drinks, not plastic

Watch out

  • Testosterone therapy shuts down sperm production. If you want kids, talk fertility first.
  • Loud snoring: sleep apnoea lowers testosterone
  • Opioids and anabolic steroids suppress your own production
  • Very low testosterone in a young man calls for a pituitary check

How it fits together

Testosterone is made on command. The brain (hypothalamus, then pituitary) sends a hormone called LH to the testes. There, Leydig cells pull in cholesterol and convert it to testosterone inside their mitochondria. The brain only sends the signal when it judges conditions safe: enough sleep, enough food, not too much stress. Once made, testosterone can be lost. Fat tissue contains aromatase, which converts it to estradiol (a form of estrogen). High insulin lowers SHBG, its carrier protein, and alcohol and some chemicals interfere along the way.

Healthy testosterone: drive, energy, muscle, libido

Low testosterone is often a signal problem (sleep, food, stress) or a loss problem (fat, alcohol), not a broken factory.

Sleep 7–9 hours

Most of the day's testosterone is released during sleep, and levels peak in the morning. In a study at the University of Chicago, healthy young men slept 5 hours a night for one week. Their daytime testosterone fell 10–15%, about what 10 to 15 years of ageing does, and they reported lower vigour. Sleep apnoea, which breaks up deep sleep all night, is a common hidden cause of low testosterone in heavier men, and treating it helps.

How: follow the Sleep guide. If you snore loudly or wake unrefreshed, get checked for apnoea.

Lose excess body fat

Obesity sets up a vicious circle. Fat tissue is rich in aromatase, which turns testosterone into estradiol. The extra estradiol tells the brain to send less LH, and insulin resistance lowers SHBG, the protein that carries testosterone in the blood. Low testosterone then makes it easier to gain fat. A meta-analysis of 24 studies found weight loss reliably raised testosterone. A low-calorie diet raised it modestly, bariatric surgery raised it about three times as much, and the more weight lost, the bigger the rise.

How: lose fat slowly with high protein and lifting so muscle is kept. Waist size is the number to watch. See Metabolic Health.

Eat enough, fat included

Testosterone is built from cholesterol, and the diet's fat seems to matter. A meta-analysis of controlled diet studies found that men on low-fat diets had lower total and free testosterone than on higher-fat diets. The effect was largest in European and North American men. Energy matters more. When food is short for long enough, as in long-term dieting, endurance overtraining or wrestling weight cuts, the brain treats it as a famine and turns reproduction down.

How: eggs, red meat, butter, full-fat dairy and wild fish. Don't run a large deficit for months. Diet in blocks, then eat at maintenance.

Lift, and don't overtrain

The short testosterone bump right after a workout is brief and doesn't build muscle by itself. Training helps testosterone over the long run by building muscle and cutting fat and insulin resistance. At the other end, men doing very high endurance volume, especially on too little food, often run low testosterone. Sport medicine calls this low energy availability.

How: lift 2–3 times a week with compound lifts, walk daily, and keep hard cardio to an amount you recover from. Take at least a day off each week.

Fix low zinc and vitamin D

Zinc is needed by Leydig cells and for making sperm, and severe deficiency causes hypogonadism. In Prasad's studies, restricting zinc in young men lowered testosterone, and giving zinc to marginally deficient older men raised it. Vitamin D: in a year-long trial in overweight men who started deficient, 3,332 IU a day raised testosterone compared with placebo. Neither helps much if you already have enough.

How: zinc from oysters (a few a week covers it), red meat and eggs. Vitamin D from sun, or 1,000–4,000 IU of D3 in winter if your blood level is low. High-dose zinc pills deplete copper, so don't take them long-term.

Drink less

Alcohol is directly toxic to Leydig cells, and heavy drinking shrinks the testes. A damaged liver clears less estrogen, so heavy drinkers often have high estrogen and low testosterone together. A night of heavy drinking also wrecks the sleep that testosterone depends on. Occasional moderate drinking has a small effect.

How: keep it to a couple of drinks, a few nights a week at most, and not close to bedtime.

Cut back on plastics

Phthalates make plastics soft and carry scent in fragrances. In US national survey data (NHANES), people with more phthalate breakdown products in their urine had lower testosterone. Among adult men, the link showed up in those aged 40–60. In boys aged 6–12, it was the strongest. In rats, these chemicals block testosterone production in the developing testes. The human evidence is observational, but cutting exposure is cheap. Heat and fat pull phthalates out of plastic and into food.

How: don't microwave food in plastic, and store hot or fatty food in glass or steel. Choose fragrance-free soap and laundry products, and eat less packaged fast food.

Worth considering

  • Ashwagandha. In an 8-week crossover trial in 43 overweight men aged 40–70, a standardised extract raised testosterone 14.7% more than placebo. Fatigue and libido didn't differ. Small, but better evidence than most herbs.
  • Manage stress. Chronically high cortisol works against testosterone. Stress & Cortisol guide
  • Creatine. It doesn't raise testosterone, but it helps you lift more. One study found a rise in DHT, which matters if you're prone to hair loss. Creatine
  • Statins. Meta-analyses find a small drop in testosterone. Weigh it with everything else. Statins: hormones

Skip, or don't count on

  • "T booster" blendsA review of 50 products found only about a quarter of the ingredients had any data showing a rise in testosterone. Some contained ingredients that lower it, and some went over safe limits for zinc and niacin.
  • TribulusTrials haven't shown it raises testosterone in men.
  • Cold plunge right before lifting for a T spikeNo trial has tested it. Cold Exposure
  • Pomegranate juice "+24% testosterone"From an unpublished abstract with no placebo group. Pomegranate
  • Testosterone therapy without testingOnly start after two low morning tests and real symptoms, and only once sleep, weight and alcohol are dealt with.
