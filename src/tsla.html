Autonomy & Robotics

TSLA — Tesla

The thesis has moved off cars. What's left is a bet on autonomous driving and humanoid robots — with an auto business funding the attempt.

01

Against the Four Criteria

Management

The most polarizing input on this list — the bull case and the bear case run through the same person. Musk's ability to recruit and to force hard engineering problems forward is real; so is the attention split across SpaceX, xAI, and politics. There's less operational buffer beneath him here than at SpaceX.

TAM

Enormous if you accept the framing: global vehicle fleet, then autonomous ride-hailing miles, then humanoid labor. Optimus in particular is sized against global labor rather than any product market, which makes the number impressive and close to meaningless. Energy storage is the quieter, more measurable piece.

Cash Flow / Position

The genuine strength, though the bill is getting bigger. Tesla generates real free cash flow and holds a large cash position, but is now guiding to over $25B of 2026 capex — funding the Fremont Optimus line, a second Optimus factory at Giga Texas, the Cybercab ramp, and a $3B semiconductor fab. Very few companies attempting things this speculative are self-funding it, but the spend is scaling with the ambition.

Execution

The two-year delivery deceleration broke, hard: Q2 2026 deliveries hit 480,126, up 25% YoY and Tesla's strongest Q2 ever, beating even the bullish estimates by more than 60,000 units on European growth and Chinese resilience offsetting a still-weak US market. Q3 held most of that: 486,532 deliveries, down 2.1% from last year's record quarter but ~25,000 above the ~462K consensus, with deliveries outrunning production by 22K as inventory came down. Energy storage was the miss — 13.7 GWh, up ~10% YoY but short of the ~15.9 GWh expected. Robotaxi has scaled to 7 US metros, Cybercab production has started, and Semi deliveries began the week of Sept 24 out of the new Nevada plant. Optimus remains the unresolved piece — still promises rather than shipping revenue.

02

Autonomous Vehicles

The nearer of the two bets, and the one with an actual revenue mechanism attached.

The economic claim

Removing the driver changes the unit economics of a mile more than any incremental improvement to the vehicle. It also converts Tesla from a company that sells a car once into one that earns on every mile that car drives — a different multiple entirely, which is the actual argument behind the valuation.

The approach

Vision-only, end-to-end neural networks, trained on fleet data. The bull case is that the fleet is an insurmountable data advantage. The bear case is that the last few percent of reliability is where vision-only approaches have historically struggled, and that competitors using lidar are already operating driverless commercially.

What I'm watching

Genuinely unsupervised operation without a safety driver, in more than a handful of geofenced cities, at a disengagement rate that regulators accept. Robotaxi has reached 7 US metros and Cybercab production has started — real progress, but still short of the unsupervised-at-scale bar. Everything short of that is a demo.

Timeline honesty

Full autonomy has been forecast as roughly a year away for a long time. I discount stated timelines heavily and weight demonstrated miles instead.

Early October: regulators

Mixed signals in the same week. In Europe, Tesla renamed Full Self-Driving to "Assisted Driving" after pushback from German regulators, while Germany separately backed a faster EU rollout of the software; the EU vote is still set for December. In the US, NHTSA's special order on the Cybercab's self-certification is unresolved (Tesla's sworn response was due Sept 30), and an adverse finding could limit where it operates. FSD v14.3.11 began rolling out Oct 8 and puts FSD, Smart Summon, and Robotaxi on one model. The Cybercab goes on show at the Paris Motor Show from Oct 12. None of this changes the bar I set above: unsupervised miles at scale.

03

Optimus & Humanoid Robots

The larger and later bet — and the one where I hold the most skepticism relative to how much of the valuation appears to reference it.

Why the TAM argument is weak on its own

"Humanoid robots address global labor" is technically true and analytically useless — it sizes an opportunity without saying anything about whether this company captures it, at what cost, or when. Under my four criteria, TAM is the ceiling check, not the thesis. Optimus passes the ceiling check trivially and fails the execution check today.

The real argument for it

The strongest version isn't the TAM — it's that autonomy and robotics share the same substrate. The perception stack, the training infrastructure, the inference silicon, and the manufacturing capability are largely common. Optimus is closer to a second application of existing investment than a separate moonshot.

The hard part

Manipulation is meaningfully harder than driving. Driving is a constrained problem with lane lines and traffic rules; general-purpose grasping in unstructured environments is not. Demonstrations of choreographed tasks say little about that.

What would change my mind

Optimus doing useful, unscripted work in Tesla's own factories at scale — measured in units deployed and tasks displaced, not in stage appearances. Internal deployment before external sales is the credible sequence.

Straight from Musk

On the Q4 2025 call (Jan 2026), Musk asked investors to be patient: roughly 300 units deployed, "primarily for learning, not productive tasks — still very much in the R&D phase." Enterprise leasing is the 2026 commercial path; a $20–30K consumer version is a 2027-and-later scenario. That's the company's own timeline, not a bear's discount on it.

September 2026 update

The Gen 3 design looks meaningfully more finished — enclosed body panels instead of exposed actuators — and Musk has confirmed it runs xAI's Grok for conversational voice control. Supply-chain reports put 2026 parts orders around 15,000 units, ramping toward ~1,000/week by late September and ~2,500/week by year-end; those are reports, not company guidance, and they're a big jump from ~300 units in January. The piece I actually care about — a public demo of unscripted factory work — is expected around late September/October. If it shows real tasks rather than choreography, this is the first update that moves Optimus from “demo” toward “deployment.”

04

The Auto Business Underneath

Its actual role

To fund the above. Vehicle sales generate the cash flow and the training data; energy storage adds a real, growing, less-discussed business. Neither justifies the current multiple alone, and neither is supposed to.

Semi — finally in volume

Tesla formally rolled out the Semi at a Sept 24, 2026 event at its new 1.7M sq ft plant in Sparks, Nevada, and began deliveries to the first customers the same week — nine years after the 2017 unveil. The plant is built for up to 50,000 trucks a year; analysts expect 5,000–15,000 in 2026. The demand signal is real: a single order for 2,500 Semis from a coalition of large shippers including Microsoft and PepsiCo landed the same week. The market shrugged — the stock closed down 1.5% on Sept 25 — which tells you Semi is priced as a sideshow to autonomy, not part of the thesis. I'd treat it the same way energy storage is treated: real, measurable, and underappreciated.

Roadster — unveiled Oct 15, and it flew

After sliding from Oct 1 on weather, the second-gen Roadster was revealed on Oct 15 at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas. The demo used thrusters and fans to climb an inverted ramp, flip, and land, and Tesla showed 0–60 mph in under a second. Musk said "success is not guaranteed" but "it will probably work," and put production in 2028, the back end of the 2027–2028 range I had before. No price was announced; the website says the flying version isn't approved for public roads. Gene Munster called it "an engineering flex with no commercial value." The stock rose about 4% intraday on Oct 16, then gave it back. It's a halo car, not a volume product — but it was built with SpaceX hardware at a SpaceX site, which is why the next section exists.

The pressure

Growth has decelerated and competition — particularly from Chinese manufacturers — has compressed pricing. The base business getting weaker while the option value gets more expensive is the uncomfortable version of this setup.

05

The SpaceX Question

Neither company has said anything publicly about a merger. Wall Street and prediction markets are pricing one anyway, and it could land before Tesla's annual meeting.

What's actually on the record

On Oct 14, Barclays said a deal could be announced before Tesla's annual meeting, which has to happen by Dec 6, 2026, and that it expects SpaceX to acquire Tesla in a stock-for-stock transaction. Barclays framed it around AI, linking Tesla's robotaxi and Optimus with SpaceX's own AI efforts, and called the Terafab chip project (Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI, reportedly costing up to $119B) a key driver. Dan Ives puts the odds of a merger before the end of 2027 near 80%. On Kalshi, odds of one before April 2027 jumped from ~30% to ~55% after the Roadster demo.

What isn't

No filing, no statement from either company, and Musk has only said any combination would have to follow the appropriate process. Barclays itself notes that announcing in late October or November could make approval at the annual meeting harder. This is an analyst scenario with a strong tailwind of circumstantial evidence (shared Terafab, a Roadster built on SpaceX hardware at a SpaceX site), not news.

Why it matters to the thesis

Terms would be everything. In a stock-for-stock deal, Tesla holders would trade a company with real free cash flow and $25B+ of capex for a share of a larger, even more capital-hungry combined entity, at a ratio set while Musk sits on both sides. SpaceX is itself down from its Oct 6 high and has just been reported to be seeking $40B of debt for chips. My four criteria apply to the combined company, not the pieces: management gets more concentrated, cash flow gets diluted by the AI buildout, and execution gets a new integration test.

What I'm doing with it

Treating it as a risk to the multiple, not a catalyst, until there's a document. Q3 earnings on Oct 21 is the first place Tesla management could be asked directly.

The trend

Institutions added roughly 379M shares gross last quarter (about 122M net of sells), and buyers outnumbered sellers by nearly 3-to-2. That's a real increase in conviction running alongside the delivery deceleration covered above, not against it.

Concentration

The top 5 holders — BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, Geode Capital, and one more — account for roughly 28% of disclosed institutional shares. That's mostly passive index managers, so a rising percentage partly reflects index weight rather than pure active stock-picking conviction.

What I'm watching

Whether active, non-index managers keep adding through the delivery slowdown, or whether the increase is mechanically index-driven. The buyer/seller count spread tells me more than the headline percentage.

07

Bear, Base & Bull Case

Q2 2026 handed both sides new ammunition — a real delivery reacceleration and an explicit admission that the bigger bet is still years out.

Bear

One good quarter, priced like a trend

If this plays out: ~$230

More analysts rate Tesla Hold than Buy even as price targets climb — a sign the multiple already reflects the delivery beat. Musk himself has now put Optimus firmly in 2027-and-later territory: ~300 units, "still very much in the R&D phase," no productive tasks yet. Meanwhile over $25B of 2026 capex is being poured into factories for products with no proven unit economics — Optimus lines, Cybercab, a $3B chip fab — while the US market stayed weak and Europe/China did the lifting. If Q2 was a one-off rather than a reversal, the multiple has nowhere to hide.

Base

The auto business bought more time

If this plays out: ~$400

Q2's +25% YoY deliveries and Q3's 486,532 (down ~2% YoY but ~25,000 above estimates) are real evidence the core business is stabilizing rather than decaying while the market waits on autonomy — it funds the longer bets for longer. Robotaxi in 7 metros and Cybercab production starting are incremental, verifiable steps, not vaporware, but nowhere near unsupervised-at-scale. Optimus stays a 2027+ call exactly as Musk now frames it. Expect the stock to keep trading on delivery and robotaxi-metro headlines quarter to quarter rather than settling into a stable multiple.

Bull

Paying up front for capacity that hasn't tipped yet

If this plays out: $600+

If the Q2 reversal is a trend, Tesla gets credit for a growing, higher-margin auto/energy base while robotaxi scales past 30 cities (Wedbush's call) on manufacturing lines and silicon that already exist rather than needing to be built later. The same fleet-data and inference substrate serves autonomy and Optimus at once, so capex spent on one isn't wasted if the other lags. Wedbush's $600 target — a $2T market cap in 2026, up to $3T bull case — is what this looks like if both bets land anywhere close to on time.

Where the stock sits today

Roughly $360–377 on Oct 16, 2026 (quotes vary by source, 52-week range $297–$499), down from $382.70 at the Oct 9 close as the Nasdaq had its worst week since June, against an average analyst target near $397 — Goldman Sachs at $375 (Neutral) on one end, Wedbush's Dan Ives at $600 (Outperform) on the other. The Roadster is now out of the way; next is Q3 earnings on Oct 21 after the close, where Goldman expects the robotaxi and Optimus outlook to matter more than the quarter itself, and where the merger question may come up. That more analysts rate it Hold than Buy despite rising targets tells you the disagreement is about the multiple, not the price. The scenario targets above are my own rough, directional estimates — not formal price targets — anchored to the sell-side range rather than a full model.

08

Risks

Valuation

Prices in successful execution of at least one, arguably both, of the speculative bets. Failure on both leaves an auto company with a compressing multiple.

Capital intensity

Over $25B of 2026 capex funding Optimus, Cybercab, and an in-house chip fab simultaneously. That's a lot of speculative spend to fund at once even from a strong balance sheet.

Key person

Attention split across multiple companies, and a public profile that has become a demand-side variable in some markets.

Merger overhang

A SpaceX combination, if it comes, would be priced in stock between two companies Musk controls. Until there's a filing, it's an uncertainty on the multiple in both directions, and terms would matter more than the headline.

Regulatory

Autonomy approval is jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction and slow. A single high-profile safety failure can reset the timeline everywhere.

Competition

Waymo is already operating driverless commercially with a different sensor approach; Chinese EV makers are pressuring the core business on price and pace.
