Milk Thistle (Silybum marianum — Silymarin)

Herb / Seed extractLiverBlood sugar

The best-known liver herb. The trials are more mixed than its reputation, but the best fit is fatty liver with scarring.

At a glance

  • Lowers blood sugar A few small trials saw lower A1c in people with type 2 diabetes. why ↓

The Research

Mechanisms, studies and evidence grades for each claim above.

May reverse liver scarring in fatty liver

Silymarin is a mixture of flavonolignans from the seed: silybin A and B (together ~50–70%), isosilybin, silychristin, silydianin, and the flavonoid taxifolin. In cell and animal models it inhibits activation of hepatic stellate cells (the cells that turn into collagen-producing myofibroblasts and scar the liver), dampens NF-κB signalling, and supports glutathione. (Animal–in-vitro)

The best human test is a 48-week, double-blind RCT in Malaysia of 99 adults with biopsy-proven NASH, using 700 mg silymarin three times daily:1

  • Primary endpoint missed: a ≥30% drop in NAFLD activity score occurred in 32.7% on silymarin vs 26.0% on placebo (not significant).
  • Fibrosis improved: 22.4% vs 6.0% had a ≥1-stage improvement in fibrosis on repeat biopsy, and 24.2% vs 2.3% had a ≥30% fall in liver stiffness. Non-invasive fibrosis scores (APRI, FIB-4, NAFLD fibrosis score) also fell on silymarin only.

So silymarin didn't clear fat or inflammation, but it may have reversed some scarring — the part of fatty liver disease that actually predicts outcomes. Because fibrosis was a secondary endpoint, the authors themselves call it unconfirmed. (Moderate — one well-run biopsy trial; secondary endpoint) The fatty liver itself is still a metabolic problem, handled by diet (see Linoleic Acid and Metabolic Health); silymarin is an adjunct.

Lowers blood sugar

In a 4-month double-blind trial in 51 Iranian patients with type 2 diabetes, 200 mg silymarin three times daily on top of standard therapy lowered HbA1c, fasting glucose, total and LDL cholesterol, triglycerides and liver enzymes compared with placebo.2 Several later small trials, mostly from Iran and India, point the same way. Proposed mechanisms include lower hepatic glucose output and less oxidative stress in β-cells. (Moderate — consistent direction, but small trials from a narrow set of centers)

Protects the liver from toxins and alcohol

Mushroom poisoning

Silibinin blocks the liver-cell uptake transporter (OATP1B3) that lets α-amanitin, the Amanita death-cap toxin, into hepatocytes. IV silibinin (Legalon SIL) is used for death-cap poisoning in Europe, and it's the clearest pharmacological action milk thistle has. But that's an IV drug at hospital doses, not a capsule. (Moderate — case series and clinical use, no RCTs; ethical constraints)

Alcoholic cirrhosis

The two classic RCTs disagree. Ferenci et al. (1989, 170 patients, 140 mg three times daily) reported better 4-year survival on silymarin, driven by the alcoholic-cirrhosis subgroup.3 Parés et al. (1998, 200 alcoholic cirrhotics, 150 mg three times daily for 2 years) found no effect on survival or disease course.4 Continued drinking in both cohorts muddies either result. (Contested)

Hepatitis C

The NIH-funded SyNCH trial is the strongest negative: 154 patients with hepatitis C who had failed interferon got 420 or 700 mg three times daily — higher than usual doses — for 24 weeks. It made no difference to ALT, viral load or quality of life.5 Milk thistle doesn't treat viral hepatitis.

How to take it

  • Form: a standardized extract labelled ~70–80% silymarin. Whole ground seed or tea gives very little, because silymarin is poorly water-soluble.
  • Dose: trials range from 140 mg to 700 mg three times daily. The positive fibrosis trial used the high end (2.1 g/day).
  • Absorption: oral bioavailability of silybin is poor (roughly 1% or less). Phytosome preparations, where silybin is complexed with phosphatidylcholine (sold as Siliphos), give several-fold higher blood levels, so a lower labelled dose can go further.
  • With food: take with a meal that contains fat.
  • Timescale: the fibrosis signal took 48 weeks. This is not an acute “liver detox” product — the idea of taking it the morning after drinking isn't what the trials tested.

Safety

Cautions: milk thistle is in the daisy family (Asteraceae), so people allergic to ragweed, chrysanthemum or marigold can react (the same caution applies to chamomile). The glucose-lowering effect can add to diabetes medication. Silymarin has weak in-vitro effects on some CYP enzymes, but clinically meaningful drug interactions haven't been shown. Across trials, adverse events were similar to placebo: mild GI upset, and a loose-stool effect at higher doses.
