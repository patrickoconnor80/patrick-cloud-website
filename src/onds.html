ONDS — Ondas Holdings

A defense-tech roll-up scaling revenue fast on a large contracted backlog — funded by heavy share issuance, which is the whole argument.

01

Against the Four Criteria

Management

The hardest of the four to underwrite here. Management is running an aggressive acquisition strategy — six acquisitions and counting — which demands integration skill on top of operating skill. They have consistently raised guidance, which is a point in their favor, but the track record is short.

TAM

Drone warfare and counter-UAS, which has gone from speculative to a procurement priority. Management points to a pipeline of roughly $4.3B across 45+ program submissions, and the $982M Army IDIQ validates relevance to long-term US drone procurement. Modern conflict has made this category structurally larger.

Cash Flow / Position

About $1.4–1.5B in cash and short-term investments with minimal debt — genuinely funded. But the company is still posting substantial losses as expenses scale with the business. Management expects EBITDA positivity during 2026; that has not happened yet.

Execution

The strongest of the four criteria for this name. 2025 revenue of $50.7M was up 605% YoY. Q2 2026 revenue hit a record $83.8M against a $68.5M consensus, and full-year guidance was raised repeatedly — from $375M to $390M to more than $525M.

Order momentum

$175M of new orders in Q2, plus a further $105M in Q3 to date. Backlog has climbed from roughly $457M earlier in the year to $613M reported, and ~$757M pro forma once the Q3 acquisitions are included.

The roll-up

Five acquisitions totaling $557M completed in 2026 — Mistral, World View Enterprises, Omnisys, then DZYNE and Cyberhawk in Q3 — expected to contribute roughly $230M of 2026 revenue. DZYNE alone adds a stated $1.5B pipeline and ~$111M of backlog.

Partnerships

Collaboration with Palantir on AI-enabled defense systems, and the LADOS command-and-control platform debuting alongside a suite of autonomous defense products.

Margins — corrected

Gross margin actually declined to 43.1% in Q2 from 49.2% in Q1, not held near 50% as the integration pace might suggest. Adjusted EBITDA loss widened nearly 5x quarter-over-quarter, from $10.9M to $50.6M, as operating expenses scaled with the acquisitions. The stock fell 5.4% on the print despite the revenue beat — the market reacting to cost, not demand.

03

The Dilution

This is the reason the share price hasn't followed the revenue. The growth is real; so is the cost of funding it.

What actually happened

Shares outstanding rose roughly 324% in a single year — diluted average share count around 462M as of March 2026. At the May 2026 annual meeting, shareholders approved raising authorized common stock from 800M to 1.2B shares, with over 50% already issued and nearly 25% reserved for higher-strike warrants. A $229M ESOP-related shelf was filed alongside.

So the company both has diluted heavily and has explicitly created room to keep doing it. That's the bear case in one paragraph.

The counterargument

Revenue growth has outpaced share issuance. On a per-share basis the business is still expanding — and the acquisitions bought with that equity are what produced the backlog. Forward EV/revenue has compressed from ~21x to single digits as estimates caught up. Whether that's cheap or a value trap depends entirely on whether the backlog converts.

Short interest

Roughly 40–44% of the float has been reported sold short — an unusually crowded bet against the dilution thesis, which makes the stock reflexive in both directions.

What I'm watching

Two things: (1) does backlog convert to recognized revenue on schedule, and (2) does share count stabilize now that the cash pile is large. If both happen, the per-share math turns quickly. If issuance continues while conversion slips, the bears are right.

The signal

Institutional shares held rose roughly 49% quarter-over-quarter — about 105M shares added — with 383 institutions increasing positions against 88 reducing, and 181 new holders initiating versus 125 exiting entirely. That's a lopsided buy/sell ratio for a name this contested.

BlackRock

BlackRock disclosed a 7.2% passive stake in July 2026. The top 5 holders control roughly 31% of disclosed institutional shares, top 10 about 47% — concentrated, but not dominated by any single fund.

How it cuts against the bear case

Roughly 40–44% of the float is reported short. Institutions net-buying into that setup while the company keeps issuing shares is a real data point for the bulls — it means the money absorbing the dilution isn't just the company's own ATM desk finding retail buyers.

05

Bear, Base & Bull Case

The market's own reaction to Q2 is the cleanest summary: record revenue and a raised guide, and the stock still fell — because the integration bill showed up faster than the backlog conversion.

Bear

The cost side broke first

If this plays out: ~$5

Record $83.8M revenue and a raised guide still sent the stock down 5.4%, because adjusted EBITDA losses nearly quintupled quarter-over-quarter ($10.9M to $50.6M) and gross margin fell from 49.2% to 43.1% in the same three months. That's the roll-up risk showing up exactly where you'd expect — six-plus acquisitions integrating at once while opex outruns even genuinely strong revenue growth. Layer on the existing 40–44% short interest and last year's 324% dilution, and this needs the backlog to convert cleanly and the margin trend to reverse, with little room for another quarter like Q2's cost overrun.

Base

Demand is intact; the market re-priced for cost discipline

If this plays out: ~$15

Revenue and backlog keep beating and guidance keeps rising ($525–550M full-year now) — the top-line half of the thesis is still working. What the market re-priced is integration cost discipline, not demand. Institutional ownership climbing (56%+ and still rising) even through this margin scare suggests professional money is underwriting the backlog-conversion story more than the quarterly EBITDA print. Expect the stock to keep trading on the gap between revenue beats and margin trend each quarter until one clearly wins.

Bull

A short-squeeze setup if margins stabilize

If this plays out: ~$22.75

If Q2's margin and EBITDA slippage prove to be a one-quarter integration spike — plausible, given five acquisitions closed inside two quarters — then a company guiding to $525–550M in 2026 revenue on a $757M pro forma backlog looks meaningfully mispriced at $9.51. Analysts already see it that way: Strong Buy consensus, average target above $19, Ladenburg's $22.75 implying more than double from here. With 40–44% of the float short against exactly that conversion thesis, one clean quarter — revenue beat and margin improvement together — could move the stock sharply on the squeeze alone.

Where the stock sits today

$9.51, against a Strong Buy consensus averaging roughly $19.42–$20.13 (Ladenburg's high at $22.75, raised Aug 17, 2026). That's one of the widest price-to-target gaps on this watchlist — the market is pricing in more execution risk than the sell side is. The scenario targets above are my own rough, directional estimates — not formal price targets — anchored to the sell-side range rather than a full model.

06

Risks

Integration

Six acquisitions in roughly a year is a lot of moving parts. Roll-ups fail on integration far more often than on demand.

Profitability

Q2 adjusted loss of $0.19/share missed the $0.10 consensus, gross margin fell to 43.1% from 49.2%, and adjusted EBITDA loss widened nearly 5x to $50.6M. Revenue beats haven't yet translated into the bottom line — if anything, the trend moved the wrong way this quarter.

Government concentration

Revenue is heavily tied to defense procurement timing and budget cycles — lumpy by nature, and politically exposed.
