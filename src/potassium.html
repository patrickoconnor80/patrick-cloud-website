Potassium

MineralFoundational

The main mineral inside your cells, and the counterweight to sodium.

At a glance

Essential · most people run low
  • Keeps nerves and muscles firing Holds the electrical charge across every cell membrane. why ↓
  • Steady heart rhythm Needed for normal heart contractions. why ↓
  • Balances sodium The two together control fluid balance. why ↓
Strong human evidence

How to take it

  • 2,600–3,400 mg a day, from food
  • Baked potato, tomato, spinach, banana, avocado, orange juice

Watch out

  • Keep supplement doses small. High-dose potassium is a heart risk.
  • Diuretics and GI illness drain it

Signs you're low

  • Weakness, cramps, constipation
  • Irregular heartbeat

Functions

  • Maintains resting membrane potential
  • Supports cardiac rhythm and muscle contraction
  • Balances sodium for fluid/electrolyte homeostasis — see Sodium
↑ back to summary

Mechanism

Works opposite sodium via the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump to maintain electrochemical gradients across the cell membrane.

↑ back to summary

Intake

Adequate intake roughly 2,600 mg/day (women) and 3,400 mg/day (men). Emphasize food sources — supplements are typically capped per dose for safety reasons (rapid IV or high-dose oral potassium is a real cardiac risk).

↑ back to summary

Deficiency effects

Low potassium (often from losses via sweat, GI illness, or diuretic medications) can cause weakness, cramps, constipation, and arrhythmias; severe hypokalemia can be dangerous.
