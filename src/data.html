Market Thermometer

One score from −100 (washed out) to +100 (overheated), built from the readings traders use to spot short-term extremes: the VIX against its multi-year range and its weekly RSI, VIX term structure, McClellan breadth oscillators, the share of stocks above their 50-day average, and SPY/QQQ momentum and extension. Updated each trading day near the close.

How to read it

The two ends do not carry equal weight. Cold readings cluster near tradeable lows: fear, breadth and momentum all wash out together and snap back fast. Hot readings are a warning, not a sell signal; overbought markets can stay overbought for months, and the backtest below shows forward returns at the hot end were still positive, just smaller. Tops tend to be processes (breadth thinning while the index holds up), while bottoms tend to be events.

Each reading is scored against fixed bands (VIX 14–28, VIX weekly RSI 35–70, VIX/VIX3M 0.82–1.02, RSI 30–70) or, for breadth and extension, its percentile over the prior three years. The McClellan oscillators are computed from S&P 1500 and Nasdaq-100 members, stand-ins for the exchange-wide $NYMO and $NAMO, and history uses today’s index members, so older breadth readings carry some survivorship bias. Not a recommendation to trade.

02

Dealer Gamma Now

A live estimate of options-dealer positioning in SPY and QQQ, with key levels also quoted in S&P 500 (SPX) and Nasdaq-100 (NDX) points, refreshed every 15 minutes during market hours. Free data covers only the ETF options; SPX and NDX index options carry more of the dealer gamma, so treat this as a partial view. Positive gamma means dealer hedging tends to dampen moves; negative means it tends to amplify them. See Learn for how to read it.

DateEventImplied move (ATM straddle)

Model estimate

Computed from delayed option chains (open interest as of the prior close; the data source is named under the tables) using Black-Scholes gamma and the usual convention that dealers are long calls and short puts. Expirations within the next week are included. Dealer books are not public, so the signs are an assumption, not an observation. Not a recommendation to trade.
