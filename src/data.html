Market Cycle

Where the broad market sits between a washout and a blow-off, and which way it is moving. The score (−100 to +100) combines fear (VIX level, VIX weekly RSI, VIX term structure), breadth (McClellan oscillators, the share of stocks above their 50- and 200-day averages) and momentum (SPY/QQQ RSI and extension). Its direction sets the phase: Decline, Bottoming, Advance, Topping. High-beta leadership and the macro backdrop sit alongside, kept out of the score. Updated each weekday evening after the close.

How to read it

The two ends do not carry equal weight. Washouts cluster near tradeable lows: fear, breadth and momentum break together and snap back fast, and the better entries have come on the turn (Bottoming) rather than the first dip. Overheated readings are a warning, not a sell signal: the index has usually kept grinding higher, but high-beta stocks (SPHB) have gone roughly nowhere over the following three months. Tops tend to be processes, with beta leadership and breadth fading while the index holds up; bottoms tend to be events.

The macro panel is about the tide, not the timing. Rates, inflation expectations, credit and the dollar move over months and do not mark tops and bottoms, but they decide how hard high-beta stocks can run once a bottom is in.

Scoring: fixed bands for VIX (14–28), VIX weekly RSI (35–70), VIX/VIX3M (0.82–1.02), Nasdaq % above 200-day (15–75%) and RSI (30–70); the McClellan oscillators, % above 50-day and SPY extension are scored by percentile over the prior three years. Breadth is computed from today’s S&P 1500, Nasdaq-100 and Nasdaq-listed members (stand-ins for $NYMO, $NAMO and $NAA200R), so it carries survivorship bias: delisted losers drop out, and the Nasdaq 200-day series runs several points above StockCharts’ version. Not a recommendation to trade.

Dealer Gamma Now

A live estimate of options-dealer positioning in SPY and QQQ, with key levels also quoted in S&P 500 (SPX) and Nasdaq-100 (NDX) points, refreshed every 15 minutes during market hours. Free data covers only the ETF options; SPX and NDX index options carry more of the dealer gamma, so treat this as a partial view. Positive gamma means dealer hedging tends to dampen moves; negative means it tends to amplify them. See Learn for how to read it.

Model estimate

Computed from delayed option chains (open interest as of the prior close; the data source is named under the tables) using Black-Scholes gamma and the usual convention that dealers are long calls and short puts. Expirations within the next week are included. Dealer books are not public, so the signs are an assumption, not an observation. Not a recommendation to trade.
