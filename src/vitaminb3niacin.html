Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

VitaminFoundationalCofactorElectrochemical
TL;DR: Vitamin B3 (niacin) is a water-soluble vitamin that functions as a precursor to NAD⁺ and NADP⁺, essential coenzymes in cellular energy metabolism and redox reactions.1

Core mechanism

Niacin converts to NAD⁺/NADP⁺, which carry electrons and enable ATP production and cellular metabolism broadly.

Energy metabolism

NAD⁺ accepts electrons across glycolysis, the TCA cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation, driving ATP production via the electron transport chain.1

Redox / cellular metabolism

NAD⁺/NADH regulate cellular redox state; NADP⁺/NADPH support biosynthesis (lipids, cholesterol) and antioxidant systems.2

DNA repair / longevity

NAD⁺ is a substrate for PARPs (DNA repair enzymes) and sirtuins (cellular regulation, stress response).3

Brain

Supports neuronal energy metabolism; NAD⁺ depletion is linked to impaired brain function.

Skin / deficiency

Niacin deficiency produces pellagra — the classic triad of dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia1 — reflecting systemic energy failure rather than a skin-specific problem.

Transport / synthesis

Can come from diet or be synthesized from tryptophan, which itself requires vitamin B6. See Vitamin B6.

Intake

Roughly 14–16 mg/day.

Top foods

  • Chicken
  • Turkey
  • Tuna
  • Salmon
  • Beef
  • Peanuts

Key insight

Vitamin B3 provides NAD⁺, a central molecule required for energy production, redox balance, and cellular repair.1
