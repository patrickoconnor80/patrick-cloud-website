Vitamin K2

VitaminFoundationalCofactor

Tells calcium where to go: into bone, and out of your arteries.

At a glance

Worth taking with D
  • Builds bone Activates osteocalcin, which locks calcium into bone. why ↓
  • Keeps arteries clear Activates MGP, which blocks calcium buildup in vessels. why ↓
Some human evidence

How to take it

  • 90–200 mcg a day
  • MK-7 form, taken daily
  • Natto is the richest food. Also eggs, dairy, liver.

Watch out

  • Matters most if you take vitamin D, which raises calcium absorption
The Research

Mechanisms, studies and evidence grades for each claim above.

Core mechanism

K2 enables γ-carboxylation of proteins, allowing them to bind calcium — this is what controls where calcium gets deposited in the body.

↑ back to summary

Bone (osteocalcin)

Activates osteocalcin, which binds calcium and incorporates it into the bone matrix.1 Without K2, osteocalcin stays undercarboxylated and inactive, reducing bone mineralization.

↑ back to summary

Vascular system (matrix Gla protein, MGP)

Activates matrix Gla protein (MGP), which inhibits vascular calcification.2 Without K2, inactive MGP allows calcium to accumulate in arteries — higher K2 status is associated with reduced arterial calcification and cardiovascular risk.3

↑ back to summary

Interaction with vitamin D

Vitamin D increases calcium absorption; K2 directs where that calcium goes (into bone via osteocalcin). Without sufficient K2, there's a higher risk of calcium ending up in the wrong place. See Vitamin D.

↑ back to summary

Deficiency effects

Undercarboxylated (inactive) osteocalcin and MGP, associated with vascular calcification and reduced bone quality.2

↑ back to summary

Forms: MK-4 vs. MK-7

  • MK-4 — shorter half-life; found in animal tissues.
  • MK-7 — longer half-life and broader tissue distribution;2 found in fermented foods (e.g., natto); more stable circulating levels. This is the form the Metabolic Health hub recommends, with the explicit caveat that MK-7's long half-life doesn't license intermittent dosing — daily intake is still needed for consistent carboxylation activity.
