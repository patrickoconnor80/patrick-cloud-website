Stearic Acid (C18:0)

CompoundExperimental

A saturated fat from beef fat and cocoa that's notably neutral on LDL.

At a glance

Fine to eat
  • Neutral on LDL Unlike palmitic acid, it doesn't tend to raise LDL. why ↓
  • Becomes oleic acid The body converts some into the main fat in olive oil. why ↓
Some human evidenceEarly / limited

How to take it

  • Dark chocolate (85%), tallow, butter, beef

Watch out

  • High-saturated-fat diets can raise LDL in some people

Functions

  • Energy source and membrane lipid component
  • Can be converted to oleic acid via SCD1 (stearoyl-CoA desaturase)
↑ back to summary

Excess effects

Context-dependent — high saturated fat intake patterns may raise LDL in some people, though stearic acid specifically is generally considered more neutral on that axis than other saturated fats (e.g., palmitic acid) when swapped calorie-for-calorie.

Deficiency effects

No known deficiency syndrome.
