Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

VitaminFoundationalCofactorAntioxidant

Carries electrons in your mitochondria and keeps glutathione recycling.

The Research

Mechanisms, studies and evidence grades for each claim above.

Core mechanism

Riboflavin converts to FAD/FMN, which carry electrons and support mitochondrial energy production.

↑ back to summary

Energy metabolism

FAD and FMN act as electron carriers in the TCA cycle (e.g., succinate dehydrogenase) and the electron transport chain (Complex I & II), transferring electrons to generate ATP.1

↑ back to summary

Redox / antioxidant system

FAD/FMN participate in oxidation-reduction reactions and help maintain cellular redox balance. Riboflavin is also required for regeneration of glutathione via the FAD-dependent enzyme glutathione reductase — linking energy metabolism directly to antioxidant defense. See Glutathione.2

↑ back to summary

Interaction with other nutrients

Works with vitamin B3 (niacin, via the NAD⁺/NADH system) and vitamin B1 (thiamine, upstream metabolism), and with magnesium for ATP activation — all part of the same integrated energy system. See Vitamin B3, Vitamin B1, Magnesium.

↑ back to summary

Deficiency

Impaired energy metabolism, presenting as fatigue, skin disorders, and inflammation (e.g., angular cheilitis).1

↑ back to summary

Transport / absorption

Absorbed in the small intestine via active transport; limited storage means it requires regular intake.

↑ back to summary

Intake

Roughly 1.1–1.3 mg/day.

Key insight

Vitamin B2 enables electron transfer in energy metabolism and supports antioxidant systems, making it essential for both ATP production and cellular protection.1
