Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine / PLP)

VitaminFoundationalCofactor
TL;DR: Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) is a water-soluble vitamin that functions primarily as pyridoxal phosphate (PLP), a coenzyme in amino acid metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis, and haem production.1

Core mechanism

B6 converts to PLP, which enables amino-group transfer and nitrogen flow — allowing amino acids to integrate into central metabolism via transamination, decarboxylation, and neurotransmitter formation.1

Key functions

1. Transamination

Amino acid + α-ketoglutarate ↔ glutamate + α-keto acid, enabling entry into pyruvate, acetyl-CoA, and TCA intermediates — this step determines whether protein carbon enters energy metabolism.

2. Nitrogen routing

Transfers amino groups via glutamate and aspartate, feeding the urea cycle and biosynthesis pathways — prevents accumulation of free ammonia.

3. Decarboxylation reactions

Glutamate → GABA; 5-HTP → serotonin; DOPA → dopamine. This is the direct link from amino acid metabolism to neurotransmitter synthesis.

4. Heme synthesis

PLP is required for δ-ALA synthase, the rate-limiting step — links amino acid metabolism to oxygen transport capacity.2

5. Glycogen breakdown

Cofactor for glycogen phosphorylase, enabling glucose release during demand.

Brain

Required for synthesis of serotonin, dopamine, and GABA — supports mood and cognition.1

Blood / oxygen system

Essential for heme synthesis, supporting red blood cell production.2

Heart / longevity

Helps regulate homocysteine levels; elevated homocysteine is associated with cardiovascular risk.3

Muscle / protein metabolism

Required for amino acid utilization and protein turnover.1

Deficiency effects

Neurological: irritability, depression, seizures. Hematological: microcytic anemia.1

Intake

Roughly 1.3–1.7 mg/day.

Top foods

  • Chicken
  • Turkey
  • Tuna
  • Potato (baked)
  • Banana

Failure points

  • Amino acids can't enter central metabolism
  • Nitrogen accumulates → toxicity risk
  • Reduced TCA intermediates → lower flux
  • Impaired neurotransmitter synthesis

Key insight

Vitamin B6 is a central metabolic cofactor that links amino acid metabolism to neurotransmitter production and oxygen transport, making it critical for brain, blood, and energy systems.1
