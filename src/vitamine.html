Vitamin E (Alpha-tocopherol)

VitaminUseful
TL;DR: Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant that protects cell membranes (especially polyunsaturated lipids) from oxidative damage.

Functions

  • Protects cell membranes from oxidative damage
  • Supports immune function

Mechanism

Scavenges lipid peroxyl radicals, limiting lipid peroxidation — vitamin C regenerates vitamin E after it's used this way (see Vitamin C). This is also the mechanistic reason membrane PUFA content matters: more oxidizable substrate raises the antioxidant demand on vitamin E, which connects directly to the hub paper's linoleic acid argument.

Intake

RDA 15 mg/day alpha-tocopherol for adults. Upper limit 1,000 mg/day for supplemental alpha-tocopherol under the U.S. DRI framework.

Deficiency effects

Rare; can occur with fat malabsorption — peripheral neuropathy, ataxia, hemolytic anemia, immune impairment.

Excess effects

High doses may increase bleeding risk via a vitamin K interaction — worth noting alongside Vitamin K1 and Vitamin K2 if combining supplements.

Top foods

  • Wheat germ oil
  • Sunflower/safflower oil
  • Sunflower seeds
  • Almonds
  • Hazelnuts
