Butyrate (Butyric Acid)

CompoundUseful
TL;DR: Butyrate is a short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) produced by gut microbes from fermentable fiber; it supports colon cell energy, barrier function, and anti-inflammatory signaling.

Functions

  • Primary fuel for colon cells
  • Supports gut barrier integrity
  • Immune modulation

Mechanism

Acts through SCFA receptors (GPR41/43/109A) and via HDAC inhibition, which affects gene expression in colon and immune cells.

Intake

No established RDA — butyrate isn't consumed directly in meaningful amounts so much as produced in the gut. The lever is sufficient fermentable fiber and resistant starch to support microbial SCFA production; individual tolerance varies.

Deficiency effects

No classic deficiency syndrome, but low fiber intake and correspondingly low SCFA production is associated with poorer gut barrier and inflammatory profiles in some settings.

Downsides

Increasing fermentable fiber too quickly to boost butyrate production can cause bloating and gas.

Top foods

  • Legumes that generate butyrate on fermentation (e.g., lentils, black beans)
  • Oats and barley
  • Onion and garlic
  • Resistant starch — cooled potatoes or rice
  • Butter/ghee — small direct amounts
