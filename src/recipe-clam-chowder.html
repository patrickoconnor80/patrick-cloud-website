Loaded Clam Chowder (garlic clams, cinnamon croutons)

Seafood15 minutesWeeknightB12

Store-bought chowder made better: extra chopped clams stirred in, garlic-butter clams on top, and cinnamon sourdough croutons instead of oyster crackers.

My recipe, built on a tub of clam chowder and a pack of chopped clams. The croutons are cut from Bread of Heaven cinnamon sourdough. The slightly sweet crunch against the briny soup works the way honey cornbread does with chowder.

What makes it work

  • Keep the clam juice. It's most of the flavor.
  • Two minutes of gentle simmer, then off the heat
  • Make the croutons first so they stay crisp
method ↓

Good to know

  • Overcooked chopped clams turn rubbery
  • Never boil the chowder, or it splits
  • Taste before salting, because it's already salty
notes ↓

Ingredients (2 bowls)

Chowder & clams

  • 2 cupsNew England clam chowder
  • ~8 ozchopped clams, with their juice (thawed if frozen)
  • 1 tbspbutter, for the garlic clams
  • 2 clovesgarlic, minced
  • pinchred pepper flakes
  • ½lemon
  • 2 tbspparsley or chives, chopped
  • to tasteblack pepper

Cinnamon sourdough croutons

  • 2 slicescinnamon swirl sourdough, cut into ¾-inch cubes
  • 1 tbspbutter
  • pinchflaky salt

Optional side

  • ½ bunchasparagus, seared in the skillet

Method

  1. Croutons first. Melt 1 tbsp butter in a skillet over medium. Add the bread cubes in one layer and toast 4–6 minutes, turning every minute or so, until golden and crisp on most sides. Sprinkle with flaky salt and move them to a plate. They crisp more as they cool. Keep the heat at medium. The cinnamon-sugar swirl burns quickly on high heat. It's the same lesson as the potato spices.
  2. Asparagus (optional). In the same skillet, sear the asparagus 4–5 minutes until blistered in spots. Salt it, add lemon, and set it aside.
  3. Warm the chowder. Heat it in a pot over medium-low until steaming, 5–8 minutes, stirring now and then. Don't boil it.
  4. Clams into the chowder. Stir in about two-thirds of the chopped clams with all their juice. Keep it at a gentle simmer for 2 minutes, just until the clams turn opaque and firm, then take it off the heat.
  5. Garlic-butter clams. Wipe out the skillet, melt 1 tbsp butter over medium, and add the garlic and pepper flakes for 30 seconds. Add the remaining clams, drained, and cook 60–90 seconds, stirring, until just firm. Squeeze in some lemon and add the parsley.
  6. Serve. Ladle the chowder into bowls, spoon the garlic clams into the center, and scatter the cinnamon croutons around them. Finish with black pepper. Add the croutons right before eating so they stay crunchy.

Nutrition notes

A month of B12 in one bowl

Clams are the most B12-dense food in USDA's data, at about 99 µg per 100 g cooked. With 3 oz of clam meat per bowl, plus the clams already in the chowder, one bowl comes to about 90 µg, more than a month of the daily target. Your liver stores the extra for later. It also covers more than the day's selenium and about half the day's iron. Clams are also on the FDA's lowest-mercury list. The B12 page covers why it matters. (Strong: USDA composition data)

It's already well salted

Between the chowder, the clam juice and the clams, a bowl comes to around 2,000 mg of sodium before you add any salt. The cinnamon bread adds a little more. Taste before salting. Most of the time it won't need any. The Sodium page explains why that's less of a worry than most people think.
