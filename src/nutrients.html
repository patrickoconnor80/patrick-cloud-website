Nutrients — Deep Dives

All 33 entries from the Health OS Nutrients database are now written up, one page each.

This paper holds the nutrient-level detail that Metabolic Health deliberately keeps out. Part IV of that paper decides which nutrients earn a place in the protocol and why (the tiering is a judgment call); this index — and the individual pages it links to — is the reference underneath: mechanism, deficiency effects, intake, food sources, citations where they exist, and how each nutrient connects to others.

Same six-label evidence grading as the rest of the series where grades are used: Strong / Moderate / Animal–in-vitro / Inferred / Contested / Speculative. Grades attach to individual claims, not whole nutrients.

Notes on this draft

Depth varies a lot by entry, and that's a direct reflection of the source database rather than an editing choice — some nutrients (glycine, vitamin C, magnesium, sodium, calcium, the B-vitamin energy-metabolism cluster, D, K1/K2) had rich prose and linked citations already in Notion; others (cysteine, copper, iron, selenium, biotin, glutamine) currently have little beyond the property fields. The "stub" pages are the honest reflection of that, not a placeholder to be embarrassed about — but copper and iron in particular are worth prioritizing given how directly they feed the collagen-synthesis mechanism already documented on the Glycine/Collagen and Vitamin C pages.
