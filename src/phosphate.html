Phosphate

MineralFoundational
TL;DR: Phosphate (phosphorus) is essential for ATP energy, cell membranes, and bone mineral; balance with calcium, vitamin D, and kidney function matters.

What it is

Phosphate (PO₄³⁻) is the biologically active form of phosphorus.

Why it matters

  • Energy — forms ATP and phosphocreatine, central to energy transfer (see Creatine)
  • Structure — part of DNA/RNA and cell membranes (phospholipids)
  • Bone — combines with calcium to form hydroxyapatite (see Calcium)
  • Buffering — helps regulate acid-base balance

Intake

RDA is roughly 700 mg/day for most adults; needs vary with growth, pregnancy/lactation, and clinical kidney/bone conditions.

Balance notes

Phosphate is tightly linked to calcium, vitamin D, PTH, and kidney function. A lot of "too much phosphate" concern in practice comes from phosphate food additives and from reduced kidney clearance rather than whole-food intake.

Deficiency effects

Uncommon with adequate diet; can occur with refeeding syndrome, alcoholism, antacid overuse, or certain disorders — may cause weakness, bone pain, and impaired energy metabolism. High phosphate is a key concern in chronic kidney disease.
