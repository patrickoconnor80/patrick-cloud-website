Methionine

Amino AcidUseful

An essential amino acid that drives methylation, the body's on/off switch for genes.

At a glance

Functions

  • Supports protein synthesis
  • Drives SAM-dependent methylation reactions
  • Feeds transsulfuration pathways via homocysteine
↑ back to summary

Mechanism

Methionine → SAM (S-adenosylmethionine) → SAH → homocysteine, which then branches into either remethylation back to methionine or transsulfuration toward cysteine and glutathione.

↑ back to summary

Intake

No single "optimal" intake target — aim for adequate protein intake. Methionine restriction protocols (used in longevity research) are experimental and not standardized for human use.

↑ back to summary

Deficiency effects

As an essential amino acid, insufficient protein intake can contribute to poor growth and tissue repair; outright deficiency is uncommon with adequate dietary protein.

↑ back to summary

Cross-reference: methionine load and glycine

Methionine restriction extends lifespan in multiple model organisms, and glycine offsets a specific cost of methionine overload — it suppresses methionine-induced hyperhomocysteinemia. See Glycine / Collagen for the citations behind that connection. (Animal–in-vitro)
